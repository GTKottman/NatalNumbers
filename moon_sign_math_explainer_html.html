☾ General Moon sign calculation guide

Calculate your Moon sign from time, place, and angle.

Your Moon sign is found by calculating the Moon’s apparent ecliptic longitude at the exact moment you were born. Once you have that angle, you place it into one of twelve 30° zodiac sectors.

1

Write down your birth data

Start with your birth city, birth date, and exact birth time. The Moon moves quickly through the zodiac, so exact birth time matters much more for Moon sign calculations, especially near sign boundaries.

Example format:
City, Country
YYYY-MM-DD
HH:MM AM/PM local time
2

Convert local time to UTC

Find the correct time zone for your birthplace on your birth date, including daylight saving time if it applied. Convert the local birth time into UTC so the sky position can be calculated from one shared global clock.

UTC time = local time − time zone offset

If local time is UTC−6:
UTC = local time + 6 hours
3

Convert the date to Julian Day

Astronomical formulas use a continuous day count instead of ordinary calendar dates. This number is called the Julian Day. It lets the calculation measure how many days have passed since a reference point.

Your UTC birth moment
→ Julian Day
→ days since reference epoch
4

Calculate the Moon’s mean position

Astronomical software first estimates the Moon’s mean longitude before applying many orbital corrections. The Moon’s motion is more complicated because it orbits Earth while Earth orbits the Sun.

L = L₀ + 13.176396° × n

n = days since the reference epoch
5

Correct for lunar perturbations

The Moon’s orbit contains many oscillations and perturbations caused by Earth, the Sun, orbital eccentricity, and orbital inclination. These corrections produce the Moon’s apparent longitude λ.

λ = L + Evection + Variation + Annual Equation + perturbations

λ = apparent lunar longitude
6

Match the angle to a zodiac sector

The zodiac is a 360° circle split into twelve equal 30° sections. Once you know λ, your Moon sign is whichever section contains that angle.

sector number = floor(λ ÷ 30)
Moon sign = zodiac[sector number]

The Moon moves fast through the zodiac.

The Moon moves about 13° per day through the zodiac. Because of that speed, the Moon can change signs in less than three days, making exact birth time extremely important.

Formula
λ ÷ 30
Result
One of 12 sectors

What birth location changes, and what it usually does not

Your birthplace is needed to determine the correct local time zone and daylight saving rule. For Moon sign calculations, exact birth time matters much more because the Moon moves rapidly through the zodiac. For the rising sign, houses, Midheaven, and horizon geometry, exact latitude and longitude become much more important.

Practical shortcut: Moon signs should almost always be calculated using exact birth time and an astronomical ephemeris because the Moon changes position quickly across the zodiac.
General method: birth data → UTC → Julian Day → lunar longitude → 30° zodiac sector.
